# Community code of conduct

## Our standard

Atlas welcomes people of different backgrounds, identities, disciplines, and
experience levels. Astronomy, graphics, accessibility, security, education,
and operations all benefit from respectful disagreement and careful evidence.

Participants are expected to:

- be kind, patient, and specific;
- critique ideas and evidence rather than people;
- welcome questions and explain unfamiliar terminology;
- respect names, pronouns, privacy, and boundaries;
- credit sources and other contributors;
- distinguish verified facts from estimates and opinion;
- accept correction and repair harm; and
- prioritise the safety and accessibility of the community.

Unacceptable behaviour includes harassment, discrimination, threats,
sexualised attention, deliberate intimidation, stalking, doxxing, publishing
private correspondence, sustained disruption, plagiarism, and retaliation
against someone who raises a concern.

## Scope

This standard applies in repository discussions, reviews, project spaces,
events, and public representation of the project. It also applies to private
conduct that creates a credible safety risk for participants.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns privately to the repository owner or the maintainers'
designated conduct contact. Do not include sensitive personal information in a
public issue. If the concern involves the normal recipient, contact another
maintainer or the hosting organisation's appropriate private channel.

Reports should include only what is useful: what happened, when/where, relevant
links or witnesses, immediate safety needs, and the desired way to communicate.
Anonymous or partial reports will be considered to the extent possible.

## Response

Maintainers will acknowledge reports as promptly as practical, limit access to
people needed to respond, avoid conflicts of interest, and communicate next
steps when it is safe to do so. They may remove content, warn a participant,
limit interaction, temporarily suspend access, permanently exclude a
participant, or refer an imminent safety threat to an appropriate service.

Decisions consider impact, pattern, power imbalance, intent only as context,
and willingness to repair harm. Retaliation or knowingly bad-faith reporting
is itself a violation; an unsubstantiated good-faith report is not.

## Maintainer responsibility

Maintainers apply this standard consistently and may reject contributions or
participation that undermine a safe, constructive community. They should
document enforcement decisions privately, retain them only as long as needed,
and review appeals through a maintainer not responsible for the original
decision where possible.
